## Fri, Oct 16, 2026 · image

I spent last Monday at Amdahl HQ talking to some of the world’s best GTM folks from 11x, Vercel, AWS, and more during SF Tech Week. Here’s what you missed:

A few things I’m convinced about paid ads I am convinced of now (after dozens of conversations):

1/ The best personalization signal is the search term someone just typed.

Someone searches “SOC 2 compliance software for startups,” clicks your ad, and lands on a generic homepage. You already know what they came for. The first line of the page should say it.

2/ AI is most useful for running more experiments, not just making more ads.

Instead of spending 3 weeks shipping one landing page, test 100 versions tailored to different search terms and see which ones convert.

3/ Anyone can generate 1,000 landing pages.

If one converts at 4% and another at 2%, you still need to know whether the gap is real, whether the conversions have had time to come in, and what caused the lift. Without that, you’re optimizing for nothing.

4/ The ad and the page are one loop.

Someone searches “affordable payroll software,” sees an ad promising simple pricing, then lands on a homepage about everything except pricing. 

Build the two together, and let the results decide what changes next.

## Sat, Oct 3, 2026 · image

The more I learn about paid ads, the more I think most companies shouldn’t be running them yet. Unless these things are true:

One thing we've become pretty opinionated about at Flint AI is that we don't actually want every company to run more ads.

In fact, we disqualify prospects pretty regularly (for the reasons that Codie A. Sanchez mentioned in her X post):

1. Paid acquisition can't fix a broken demand engine.

If you haven't figured out how to get customers through content, word of mouth, partnerships, outbound, community, etc., I'm not convinced putting more money into Google will solve the underlying problem.

That doesn't mean you need $5-10M in revenue first. You should just understand why people are buying before trying to scale the number of people buying.

2. ROAS isn't profitability.

Say you spend $100k on ads and generate $400k in revenue. That's a 4x ROAS, and it looks pretty great inside Google Ads. But that isn’t the same as profit. 

You can have a 4x ROAS and still have a terrible business.

3. Blended ROAS is also a trap.

Your first $10k might generate $50k, the next $10k $30k, and the next $10k $20k. Your blended ROAS still looks great even as each additional dollar gets worse.

The real question is: if I put another $1 into this channel, how much incremental profit does it create?

That's harder to calculate, but it's much closer to the decision you're actually making.

–

When someone wants to use Flint, we try asking more than just "How can we make your Google Ads account perform better?"

Before optimizing an account, we try to understand the business behind it: CAC, margins, cash payback, contribution LTV, and what the next dollar of spend actually looks like.

Sometimes the answer is spend more. Sometimes it's fix the economics first. And sometimes the right answer is don't run ads yet.

But I think that's the distinction that matters the most in performance marketing.

## Fri, Sep 4, 2026 · image

If I quit Flint AI and joined a startup as Head of Marketing tomorrow, I wouldn't spend a dollar on Google Ads until these 3 things were true:

1) Your business can afford to learn.

Google Ads doesn't magically give you a profitable CAC on day one.

Sometimes you start with a CAC that's way too high, and that's okay if you have enough runway to learn which audiences, keywords, messaging, and landing pages actually convert.

Maybe your CAC starts at $5,000 and eventually gets down to $1,500. If a customer is worth $5,000+, that learning process can be worth paying for.

2) You have a reason to believe the market will respond to your positioning.

Google Ads can be a great way to test messaging.

You can put different value propositions in front of people who are already looking for a solution and quickly learn what gets clicks, what converts, and what doesn't.

But if you haven't figured out who you're selling to or why they should care, Google Ads can turn into an expensive way to discover that.

3) There needs to be enough search demand or you need to create it.

Google Ads works best when people are already searching for the problem you're solving.

If 10,000 people search for your category every month, there's plenty of demand to capture and optimize against.

If 10 people do, you can have the world's best Google Ads strategy and still won't get much volume.

So before spending, figure out:

How many people are actually searching for what I sell?

Use Google Keyword Planner, Semrush, etc. to estimate the size of the existing demand.

And if there isn't much search volume, that's not necessarily a reason not to advertise - it may mean your first job is to create the demand before trying to capture it.

## Tue, Jul 28, 2026 · image

I know so many (Series A+) founders who burn $10,000/month on paid ads before they're ready. This is what I always tell them:

Paid ads only work once you have these 3 things:

1/ You solve an extremely painful problem

Ads cannot create demand… they help people who already have a problem discover your solution.

If nobody naturally wants what you're selling, advertising becomes incredibly expensive.

A simple test: if prospects aren't already searching for your category, competitors, or the problem you solve, paid search is going to be an uphill battle.

2/ You have an irresistible offer

This goes far beyond pricing. It's your positioning, differentiation, guarantee, urgency, and the outcome you promise.

A great offer gives people a compelling reason to choose you over every alternative. 

If your ad says "AI recruiting platform", but your competitor says "Hire engineers in 14 days or don't pay," your competitor will almost always get the click.

3/ You have a killer landing page that converts

Your landing page should communicate your value clearly, build trust with social proof, answer objections, and make the next step feel obvious.

Even a small lift in conversion rate (say 3% to 5%) can dramatically reduce your customer acquisition cost without spending another dollar on ads.

Tl;dr the flow is: Demand → Offer → Conversion.

Use Flint AI for step 3 - an AI agent for Google ads that makes killer landing pages that convert.
