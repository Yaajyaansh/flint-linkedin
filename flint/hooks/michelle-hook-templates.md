# Michelle's Hook Templates

Michelle's own patterns extracted from her highest-performing posts. These are the structures that have worked for her specifically — not generic templates.

---

## Structure 1: Personal action + counter-expectation

The hook states something she did, then introduces a surprising contrast or reason. Creates a curiosity gap without giving the payoff.

```
I [did something specific]. [Reframing qualifier that creates tension].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "I turned down a return offer from Meta at 19. Not because it wasn't good. But because I'd realized something that summer that made the offer irrelevant…"
- "I raised from Sheryl Sandberg to make marketing autonomous. Here's where I think marketing is headed…"
- "I got accepted into Singapore's top medical school. But I turned it down."

**When to use:** Career decisions, pivots, choices that look irrational from the outside. The "turned down" version consistently outperforms the "accepted" version.

---

## Structure 2: Specific scene at a specific time

Opens in a real moment, at a real place, with real people. No thesis. The scene carries the reader into the post before the argument arrives.

```
[Specific time/place/event], [specific observation or action].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "At our Devil Wears Prada 2 screening, every marketing leader kept asking which creative agency built our event page."
- "I biked across Manhattan 3 days in a row to visit 15 of NYC's top AI startups."
- "When I interned at Slack, then-CEO Stewart Butterfield personally responded to every product idea I shared."
- "Yesterday I came to work wearing a burgundy top."

**When to use:** Events, customer moments, office scenes, travel, any story where the setting itself adds credibility. Especially effective for posts where the scene IS the proof.

---

## Structure 3: Easy/Hard contrast

States a common thing that sounds simple, then immediately names what's actually difficult. The contrast is the hook — no setup needed.

```
It's easy to [common action]. It's hard to [the real challenge].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "It's easy to vibe code a landing page. It's hard to build a system where pages create themselves."
- "The easiest AEO + SEO win is comparison pages."

**When to use:** Product positioning, capability demonstrations, market education. Works whenever Michelle wants to distinguish Flint from simpler alternatives without making a direct comparison.

**Contrarian hooks don't land without a personal story attached.** This structure is a claim, not a scene — it needs a specific moment (something Michelle built, saw, or did) right behind it, or it reads as a generic opinion. If there's no personal anchor available, don't force this hook.

---

## Structure 4: The number construction

When the post can anchor to a single number, that number IS the hook. No preamble needed.

```
[Big specific number written out in full] + [unexpected concrete action].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "The best $120,000 we've spent at Flint AI is our office."
- "I gathered growth leaders from companies worth over $6,000,000,000 and I gave them homework."
- "We raised $5,000,000. Vercel filmed our office."
- "I've been waiting eight years to make this hire."
- "144 LinkedIn posts. $5,100 in ad spend. 138 booked meetings."

**Rules:**
- Write out zeros. "$6,000,000,000" forces a slower read than "$6B".
- The number should be surprising — either unexpectedly large, unexpectedly small, or unexpectedly specific.
- Pair the number with an action that seems mismatched (gave them homework, filmed our office, make this hire).

**When to use:** Milestones, investments, results, wait times, customer counts. Any post where a number exists and would stop a scroll.

## Structure 5: Scene with named people

Opens by naming specific people, what they were wearing or doing. Photo reinforcement and specificity in one move.

```
[Name] [specific physical detail]. [Name] [specific physical detail or action].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "Naomi had on a burgundy jacket. Chris showed up in a burgundy t-shirt and matching socks."
- "@Leona and @Chris were discussing our skill for always fetching and formatting logos for logo walls. @Max and @Sam were discussing Q2 product priorities."
- "my friend @RattrayAlex just sold his company to anthropic 🎉 i love it when good people win. this photo is from 2024 in LA: he flew across the country to ask me…"

**Rules:**
- Full @ tags where possible.
- Physical details (clothing, location) tie to the photo.
- Works best when paired with a photo where you can see the people described.

**When to use:** Team moments, events, office scenes, friend announcements, anything where a photo is attached. Makes the post feel like a real dispatch, not a curated highlight.

## Structure 6: Era-naming or hot take

States a claim about where an industry or era is headed. Designed to be reshared because it puts a name on something people already feel.

```
This is the era of [claim]. / Hot take: [claim]. [Three named examples that prove it].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "When the cost of code goes to zero, marketing is your only advantage."
- "This is the era of the one-person marketing team."
- "Hot take: every business function (not just marketing) should be able to ship to the marketing site."

**Rules:**
- The claim must be specific enough to be debatable.
- Follow with three concrete examples immediately. An unsubstantiated take dies.
- Era-naming tweets get reshared because people want to be on the right side of the trend.
- **Contrarian hooks without a personal story don't land.** A hot take stated as pure industry opinion reads as generic no matter how sharp it is. Ground it in something Michelle actually did, built, or saw firsthand — the take should feel earned by experience, not just asserted.

**When to use:** Product launch tweets, market moments, reaction to industry news. Works especially well on X where reshare is the metric.

## Structure 7: Breaking news + personal proximity (the reunion hook)

Opens with a major, verifiable external event that has nothing to do with Michelle directly — an acquisition, a funding round, an IPO — then immediately collapses the distance with a personal connection from years earlier. The gap between "this huge public thing" and "I was there before it was anything" is the hook.

```
[Major external event, named companies, real number]. ~[N] years ago I [specific personal connection to it].
```

**Michelle's examples:**
- "Cursor just sold to SpaceX for $60B. ~6 years ago I met them at a startup camp by Neo."

**When to use:** When a real external event (acquisition, IPO, funding announcement) intersects with Michelle's own history — a past meeting, event, or relationship. Especially strong when the post can then unpack a web of other people from that same moment who also went on to something notable — see the "Reunion / web-of-outcomes" template in `flint/writing/patterns.md`. This differs from quote-tweeting a live announcement (see `top-posts.md`, "Run, don't walk to Neo residency"): here the news is unrelated to Flint and the personal connection predates it by years, not days.
