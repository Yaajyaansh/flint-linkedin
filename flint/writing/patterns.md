# patterns.md — Michelle Lim / Flint

## Hook: the three opening structures that consistently work

### a) Personal action + counter-expectation

"I turned down a return offer from Meta at 19. Not because it wasn't good. But because..."
"I raised from Sheryl Sandberg to make marketing autonomous."

### b) Specific scene at a specific time

"Yesterday I came to work wearing a burgundy top."
"At our Devil Wears Prada 2 screening, every marketing leader kept asking which creative agency built our event page."
"I biked across Manhattan 3 days in a row to visit 15 of NYC's top AI startups."

### c) Easy/Hard contrast

"It's easy to vibe code a landing page. It's hard to build a system where pages create themselves."
"The easiest AEO + SEO win is comparison pages."

None of these announce, celebrate, or state the thesis. The thesis arrives after the scene or contrast has done the work.

## Hook: the number construction

When a post can anchor to a single number, that's the hook.

Template: [big specific number] + [unexpected concrete action]

- "The best $120,000 we've spent at Flint AI is our office."
- "I gathered growth leaders from companies worth over $6,000,000,000 and I gave them homework."
- "We raised $5,000,000. Vercel filmed our office."

Write out the zeros. "$6,000,000,000" forces a slower read. "$6B" doesn't.

---

## Hook: the scene-with-named-people construction

Name teammates, customers, dinner guests. Full @ tags where possible.

"Naomi had on a burgundy jacket. Chris showed up in a burgundy t-shirt and matching socks."
"@Leona and @Chris were discussing our skill for always fetching and formatting logos for logo walls. @Max and @Sam were discussing Q2 product priorities."

Named people do three things: specificity, photo reinforcement, and proof that the post is grounded in a real moment.

## Content-type templates

### Launch
[Era-defining claim]. Introducing [product]. [What it does in one sentence].
We're already [proof verb] for [Customer 1] and [Customer 2]. [CTA].

### Hot take
Hot take: [universal claim]. [Example 1]. [Example 2]. [Example 3].

The three examples must name a company, role, or action. An unsubstantiated take dies. A take backed by three named specifics survives. Pair with a video or thread when possible.

### Tactic (tweet)
[Outcome]. Here's how to [task]. In [tight time window].

Example: "Here's how to make 100 pages. In 4 minutes."

### X Article
Use when the idea needs depth a tweet or thread can't carry. Three triggers: a detailed how-to with a real personal system, founder operational advice from direct experience, or a take on a trending story that requires explanation to land.

X Articles are upranked by the algorithm. They drive far higher impressions relative to likes than regular tweets.

Title structures that work:
- "How I [verb] [big result] in [timeframe/context]"
- "How [Person] and I [avoid/handle] [specific named problem]"
- "How [subject] [outcome verb] [threat]"
- "[I did X in N]. Here's my system for [outcome]."

Topics that belong in an X Article, not a tweet or thread:
- Growth lessons from Warp or Flint with a full system behind them
- Founder operational advice (co-founder dynamics, hiring, city visit systems)
- Takes on a viral moment or hot trend that need more than 280 characters to be credible

Header image is required and must be editorial. Flag Michelle before publishing. Cannot be scheduled.

The article body must fully deliver on the title. If the title says "here's my system," the article must include the complete system.

### Hire announcement
I've been waiting [N] years to make this hire.
[Name] and I [past connection].
Today they join [company] as [role], coming from [pedigree].

Lead with what makes the person exceptional, not the job title. Hire posts still need a strong hook.

### Personal milestone
[Milestone]. It's been [N years] since [origin].
First at [place 1], then [place 2], and now [current].
[One-line conviction].

### Monthly or quarterly recap
First paragraph: three short sentences, each a milestone. Second block: bulleted list of the rest. Closer: one line about what's next.

### Friend's news
Lowercase + emoji + a memory the news doesn't have access to.

my friend [Name] just [news]. this photo is from [time/place]: they...

### Cultural moment (outage, viral chart, major announcement)
Post within the hour. Two short sentences. No CTA. Let the timing be the post.

### Event recap
Logos in line one. Takeaways in the middle. Callback to the event theme in the closer.

### Case study
Lead with the customer's human angle, then the result.

Minimum specificity: the verb, the artifact, and the count.

Bad: "What would've taken me weeks was done in minutes."
Good: "Flint let me drop in a CSV and spin up hundreds of tailored pages."

## Closing lines

The closing line must distinguish, not describe.

"On-brand landing pages at scale" = description. Forgettable.
"We didn't build a page builder. We built programmatic marketing infrastructure." = distinction. Memorable.

Template: name the obvious category. Then say what you're not.

If the closing line could appear in any competitor's post, rewrite it. If it sounds assembled, cut it. Michelle's voice doesn't close clean. It stops when the point is made.

## X tweet vs X Article vs LinkedIn

**X tweet:** one sharp thing, 280 characters, no setup, no caveats. Start with the contrarian thesis.

**X Article:** thought leadership or a full system. Upranked by the algorithm. Use when the tweet version would be too thin to be credible. Editorial header image required.

**LinkedIn:** credibility and personal narrative. Longer is fine when broken into short paragraphs with white space. The hook creates a curiosity gap. The body fills it.

Same idea, three versions:
- LinkedIn: "Claude Code added an official plugin for shipping landing pages. Here's how to use it to ship dozens of pages in minutes..."
- X tweet: "The dirty secret of Claude Code for marketing: most marketers can't get past the setup."
- X Article: "How apps don't get killed by Claude" — full explanation of the assumption, the risk, and the complete system for avoiding it.

## What the performance data says

Based on real posts, Nov 2025–May 2026.

### Do more of
- Lowercase casual posts on personal or memey topics. They consistently outperform polished posts in that register.
- Cultural-moment posts. "Claude is down" hit 228 likes where typical posts get around 50.
- Backstory hire posts. The Naomi post (226 reactions) is the best LinkedIn post in six months.
- Hot takes that name three concrete examples in the very next sentence.
- Specific-number tactical posts ("100 pages in 4 minutes"). These get reposted because they're useful.
- One-liner shitposts. Founders rarely write them. It's an edge.
- X Articles for thought leadership. The algorithm upranks them significantly — 41 likes can produce 15K impressions.

### Do less of
- Long product update threads. "Flint is live on ProductHunt" with four bullet points got 20 likes. "We dropped the try" (3 lines) got 130.
- Recap posts that don't open with the strongest number.
- Question posts without a take attached. They barely move.

### To test next
- Bookmark-bait: "I just X. Here's the full playbook." as a numbered checklist, not a video.
- A standing weekly format to compound. Right now every post stands alone. Recurring formats train followers to look for you.

## The announcement trap

Announcement-shaped hooks consistently get rewritten.

Before: "The biggest launch in Flint AI's history. Today, I'm excited to share..."
After: "It's easy to vibe code a landing page. It's hard to build a system where pages create themselves."

When the post needs to be an announcement, hide it behind an observation. Observation-first, scene-first, or contrarian-first. Never announcement-first.
