### Product Hunt Launch

Ghostwriter draft: "The biggest launch in Flint AI's history. Today, I'm excited to share that we're officially live on Product Hunt. Every marketing and growth team can now launch on-brand landing pages at scale for every campaign, ad, and prospect."

Michelle's version: "It's easy to vibe code a landing page. It's hard to build a system where pages create themselves. Our customers:
- Auto-generate a landing page every time someone mentions a new use case for their product on Reddit, via our API, no human involved
- Embed Flint into their sales workflow so every outbound prospect gets a personalized page before the first call
- Make and optimize pages for every ad and keyword. Graphite doubled their conversions and influenced 7 figures in ARR. Tandem tripled their conversions.

We didn't build a page builder. We built programmatic marketing infrastructure."

What changed: announcement frame replaced by positioning argument. Customer bullets carry the proof. Closing line names the category, then says what it isn't.

### Founder-Led Content (May 2026)

Ghostwriter draft: "My biggest regret as a founder: not posting earlier in my startup journey. I didn't start posting on LinkedIn until three months into building @Flint AI. Since then, it's become our highest ROI growth channel..."

Michelle's feedback: "All very generic. No concrete or personal examples."

Michelle's version: "My biggest regret as a founder: not posting earlier in my startup journey. 'Yes, post even before you have a product.' That was what my friend @Thenuka Karunaratne told me just 3 months into building. 'But no! I don't have a product yet!' He looked at me like I was insane. 'The story of every hire. How you picked your idea. How you got your first five customers. People want to know that.' Here's what I was wrong about: I thought posting required something finished. A launch. A milestone. A win I could point to. But the stories people respond to most are from before anything was certain. The first customer who said yes. The engineer I'd been waiting eight years to hire. Some of that early content led directly to our first hires and customers. If you're early and think you have nothing to post about, you're sitting on the most interesting content you'll ever have."

What changed: the abstract argument replaced by a real conversation with a named person. Dialogue carries the thesis instead of stating it. "As a founder, you are the face of the brand" cut entirely. Closing lands on a specific claim, not an assembled summary.

Key pattern: dialogue can do credibility work without a number or metric. A named person + a real exchange is enough of a scene to earn the thesis.

Banned structures caught in the original: "Here's why I treat X as non-negotiable" and "As a founder..." opener. Both banned.

## AI-sounding closers (negative reference)

These passed initial review and got rewritten. Use as a failure checklist.

"The throughline isn't discipline. It's that ADHD forced Alex to externalize everything willpower was supposed to handle." — Problem: assembled. Sounds like a summary slide.

"Maybe we're the ones doing it wrong." — Problem: performed humility. Cut or replace with a specific claim.

"It sounds slower. It's not. 'I'll get to this' is a graveyard." — Problem: contrived three-beat reversal. Pick one sentence.

"External presence is the forcing function." — Problem: slide deck language. Say what it actually does.

"Every piece of content you put out is a chance to build brand trust at scale." — Problem: generic conclusion. No scene did the work to earn it. Replace with what actually happened as a result.

Pattern: AI drafts reach for a clean closing formulation. Michelle's voice doesn't close clean. It stops when the point is made.

## Anti-example archetypes

Four failure modes that come up repeatedly. If a draft pattern-matches to any of these, rewrite.

**Thought Leader 101**
"Startups are a marathon, not a sprint. Success is about grit, hustle, and staying hungry. Here are three lessons I've learned on my journey to the top."
Why it's wrong: overused platitudes, empty inspiration, self-congratulatory.

**VC Bro Energy**
"Just closed a huge hire and locked in our next GTM strategy. Major ops unlock. SF is wild rn. If you're not building in public, what are you even doing?"
Why it's wrong: braggy buzzwords, performative.

**Overly Academic / Jargony**
"Incentive misalignment between principal stakeholders and early-stage contributors often leads to asymmetrical risk distribution and long-term cultural fragility."
Why it's wrong: too much jargon, no heart. Sounds like an executive MBA.

**AI Default Rhythm**
"Building something memorable isn't about clicks. It's about taste. It's not just brand. It's identity. And in today's market, taste is the real moat."
Why it's wrong: every sentence is "not X, it's Y" with contrast clauses. ChatGPT default rhythm when asked to write LinkedIn content. Any draft with two or more of these patterns in close proximity is an automatic rewrite.

## Cultural references: welcome vs. avoid

Cultural references should earn their place. They signal taste, not name-drops. Don't shoehorn them in.

**Welcome:** Orwell, Marx (cited in her own writing), technical protocols (SMTP, OSI layers), startup/tech history (Vercel, Sentry, Robinhood, Cursor), SNL / Kate McKinnon, quiz show energy, "gird your loins" and similar movie quotes when contextually relevant.

**Avoid:** Generic business-book references (Simon Sinek, Patrick Lencioni, James Clear), TED-talk vocabulary, AI buzzwords as shorthand ("synergies," "leverage," "10x"), "thought leader" attribution moves.

## External expert feedback

These are from a LinkedIn expert reviewing specific posts. Same weight as Michelle's own comments.

**Carousel critique**
Post: Claude Code scheduled tasks carousel
Feedback: "Your carousels are no bueno. They don't tell me how to do anything. They get saved when they're usable. Who are you targeting with them? The way I think about educational content is: how much work does the reader have to do between reading this and implementing it? Less work = better content. Make it idiot proof. You showed me proof it exists in this one but not how I can do it myself."

The test for any educational post: how much work sits between reading it and doing it? If the answer is "a lot," the post is proof, not instruction. Proof posts get likes. Instruction posts get saves and reposts.

**Hook-body mismatch**
Post: Viktor setup post
Feedback: "You don't deliver on the promise in the hook. Promise = 'Here's exactly how we set ours up and what we use it for.' Your answer = 'First, install getviktor.com.' That is not making good on what you promised me."

The hook set up a complete how-to. The body opened with an installation step. Those are not the same thing. If the hook says "here's exactly how," the body needs the full how: the setup, the configuration, the actual use cases. Not a starting point the reader has to finish themselves.
