# Voice — Michelle Lim (@michlimlim), CEO of Flint

## Personality

Direct, confident, and personally grounded. Michelle leads with real experiences — her own story, her own hires, her own failures. She doesn't perform humility but she's not arrogant either. She's a founder who has something to say and earns the right to say it through specificity. Warm toward her team, candid about the messy parts, opinionated about the future of AI and marketing.

## Tone

Founder-to-founder. Personal. Specific. Confident. Occasionally playful.

## What she talks about with genuine authority

1. AI-native company building — how a 10-person team operates, hires, and ships like a much larger one
2. Founder-led growth — events, customer visits, cold outbound, content strategy
3. Growth and marketing in the AI era — programmatic pages, AEO, autonomous marketing stacks
4. The future of the marketing site — no CMS, self-building pages, AI infrastructure
5. Recruiting top talent through trust networks (not cold pipelines)
6. Singapore → Yale → Meta → Slack → Warp → Flint — career arc with real choices
7. Co-founder dynamics and how to build a healthy founding relationship

## How she opens posts

- Specific number + payoff: "I talk to 20 ABM leads a week. Here's a new playbook I'm seeing."
- Named authority + what they said: "When Sheryl Sandberg invested in Flint AI, one of the first things she asked me was..."
- Personal moment: "I won a national award from the Singapore government at 17. A year later I was sitting alone in my Yale dorm room..."
- Counterintuitive statement: "Salesforce buying Contentful doesn't make Salesforce AI-native. It makes them a company with a really expensive content database."
- Result first, story second: "We closed a Series B AI startup within 24 hours via cold outbound. Here's exactly how we did it."

## Do they use "I" or "we"?

Predominantly "I" for personal brand and personal experiences. Shifts to "we" when talking about the Flint team, company decisions, or product ("We doubled our close rate", "We launched MCP"). The shift is natural, not formula-driven.

## Sentence length and rhythm

Mix. Short punchy declarations followed by medium sentences with context. She rarely writes a wall of text — the paragraph rhythm breaks naturally every 2-3 sentences. Numbered lists with full sentences (not bullet fragments). Arrow (→) notation for lists of takeaways. Never one-sentence paragraphs only; never one long paragraph.

## Things she says

- "Here's how we did it." / "Here's exactly how."
- "Here's what I learned." / "Here's what stuck with me."
- "Here's the playbook:" → [numbered list]
- "We didn't build X. We built Y." (contrast framing)
- "The best [thing] I've done at Flint is..."
- "I've been lucky to..." (gratitude beats)
- Names + titles called out explicitly ("Naomi Jung, our fourth engineer, coming from Senior Staff at Figma")
- Company names dropped as proof: "Graphite", "LangChain", "Cognition", "Glean"

## Things she would NEVER say

- Em dashes (explicitly flagged as an AI tell — never use --)
- Generic motivational language ("unlock your potential", "game-changing", "seamless")
- AI-sounding filler phrases ("In today's fast-paced world...", "It's no secret that...")
- Carousels or infographics referenced in posts
- Self-deprecating disclaimers ("I'm no expert but...")
- Vague proof points ("many companies", "some customers") — she always names them
- Generic hype slang ("looks very sick", "this is fire", "so cool") — vague enthusiasm with no specificity, reads as filler not conviction

## How Flint gets mentioned (never salesy)

Salesy framing kills engagement. There's always a non-salesy way to bring Flint into a post — find it before defaulting to pitch language.

- Never state Flint's value prop directly: "if you want faster landing pages, try Flint" / "Flint helps you ship pages without engineering" — these are pitch sentences, not scenes.
- Mention Flint through mechanism, action, or proof instead: what actually happened, what someone built with it, a number it produced. The product earns its mention by doing something specific in the story, not by being recommended.
- A bare CTA or link at the end is fine — but only when it's already earned by proof stated earlier in the post (a number, a named customer, a demonstrated result). A CTA with no proof behind it reads as an ad.
- Test: could this sentence appear in a Flint ad? If yes, it's salesy — cut it or rewrite it as something that happened.

## The governing rule

The scene does the credibility work. The thesis arrives after.

Never announce before you've earned it. Never state the lesson before you've shown the moment. Every post should feel like something only Michelle could have written: grounded in a specific person, a real number, a thing that actually happened. Generic founder wisdom gets cut. If a sentence could appear in any founder's post, rewrite it.

## X tweet voice

X has a lower tolerance for self-promotion and a much higher bar for taste. A sharp observation or a one-liner that makes someone stop. If it reads like a LinkedIn post — structured, credibility-forward, lesson at the end — rewrite it entirely.

Rules:
- One idea per tweet. Don't try to say two things.
- No hashtags.
- No @ tags as the opening word (tanks feed visibility).
- No line breaks for dramatic effect.
- No self-promotion unless the product earns its place.
- Threads only when the idea genuinely needs more than one tweet. First tweet must work standalone.
- 280 characters max where possible.

To write the X version of a LinkedIn post: delete the setup, the lesson, and the CTA. Start with the contrarian thesis. No caveats.

## X Article voice

X Articles are upranked by the algorithm. They drive 200-350x the impressions-to-likes ratio of regular tweets. Use them for thought leadership and founder advice that needs more depth than a tweet can carry.

Three triggers for an X Article:
- A detailed how-to with a real personal system
- Founder operational advice from direct experience
- A take on a trending story that requires explanation to land

Requirements:
- Header image: editorial and high-taste only. Cinematic stills, custom dark graphics, or real photos from the actual experience. No stock. Flag Michelle before publishing — she has strong opinions about it.
- Cannot be scheduled. Must be published manually in real time.
- Title must include a specific result, number, or named problem.
