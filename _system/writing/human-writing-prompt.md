# Human Writing Prompt

Use this as the core instruction set when writing any LinkedIn post.

---

## Core Principle

Write the way the client actually talks. Not how they wish they talked. Not how a marketing agency would write for them.

Read their published posts first. Find the patterns:
- What sentence length do they naturally use?
- Do they use contractions? (usually yes — "I've" not "I have")
- How do they start sentences?
- What's their ratio of facts to opinion?
- Do they swear occasionally? Use humor? Stay formal?

Mirror that. Then add the hook methodology and formatting on top.

---

## Writing Rules

**1. Write out loud.**
Before including any sentence, say it in your head. If it sounds like something a human would say in conversation, keep it. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post, rewrite it.

**2. Earn every word.**
No sentence exists for atmosphere. Every line either adds information, creates curiosity, or advances the argument. Cut anything decorative.

**3. Vary sentence length.**
Short sentences carry weight. Longer sentences — the kind with a comma or two — let you build context before landing the point. Mix them. Monotony kills engagement.

**4. Be specific.**
"We grew revenue" → "We went from $12k MRR to $89k MRR in 4 months."
Specificity is credibility. Vague claims slide off. Specific claims stick.

**5. Never explain the point before making it.**
Don't set up what you're about to say. Just say it.
Bad: "I want to share something I learned about cold email."
Good: "Cold email stopped working for us in Q2. Here's what replaced it:"

**6. One idea per post.**
Don't combine two insights into one post. Pick the better one. The other becomes the next post.

**7. The ending is as important as the hook.**
The post should end with either:
- A concrete CTA (comment, DM, follow)
- A reflection that lands the point
- A question that invites real responses (not rhetorical)

Use `—` before the final line when it's a CTA or key takeaway.

**8. Only people can be active subjects.**
People do things. Abstract nouns do not.

When writing, make sure the subject of the verb is a person or a group of people (leaders, teams, employees, founders, managers, buyers), not an abstract concept.

Avoid:
- "Clarity drives execution."
- "Good leadership is deliberate."
- "Accountability builds trust."

Do instead:
- "Teams execute better when they're clear on the plan."
- "CEOs who lead deliberately get more out of their teams."
- "Managers build trust by owning their mistakes."

## Voice Calibration (per client)

Before writing, load `clients/[client]/context/voice.md` and `clients/[client]/posts/published/`.

Answer these before writing:
- What topics does this person talk about with genuine authority?
- What's their default emotion when posting — excited, measured, frustrated, proud?
- Do they use "I" or "we"? (personal brand vs. company brand)
- How long are their posts typically?
- Do they share numbers openly or keep things vague?

Write from inside that voice. Don't write a post "for" them. Write as them.

---

## What To Never Do

- Don't start with "I'm excited to share..."
- Don't end with "What do you think? Drop a comment below!"
- Don't use bullet points for everything — prose is more human
- Don't bold random words for emphasis — bold is structural
- Don't reference "this post" or "this content" in the post itself
- Don't write a conclusion that summarizes what you just said

---

## Using published posts while writing (mandatory)

Before writing a single word of the draft, do this:

1. Open `clients/[client]/posts/published/`
2. Find 2-3 posts that are most similar in tone, topic, or format to what you're about to write
3. Read them fully — not to extract ideas, but to hear the rhythm

Then answer:
- How long are their posts typically? Match that unless there's a reason not to.
- How do they open the body after the hook? Do they jump straight to the point or set a scene?
- How do they use line breaks — tight and punchy, or longer paragraphs?
- What's their closing move — a question, a CTA, a reflection?
- Do they use "I" or "we" in this type of post?
- What specific words or phrases keep appearing? Use them naturally.

Write the draft as if you'd read 50 of their posts and absorbed how they think. The goal isn't to sound like them — it's to BE them for this draft.

**What to never do:**
- Don't borrow a story or fact from a published post
- Don't use the same angle as a recent post (check dates — avoid anything from the last 60 days on the same topic)
- Don't average their voice into something generic — if they're sharp and direct in their best posts, write sharp and direct
