## Filler words

Strip "like" as conversational filler in all written contexts. Natural in Michelle's speaking voice (Slack, calls, transcripts) but absent from her written voice. If you catch yourself writing "it's like X" or "they're like Y," rewrite without it.

## Additional Banned Phrases (from founder)

- "it's worth noting" / "it's important to note" / "it should be noted" / "keep in mind that"
- "the real X is" / "what this is really about" / "what X really means"
- "here's why that matters:" / "here's what most people miss:"
- "and that changes everything" / "and this is key" / "which is exactly why this matters"
- "at its core" / "fundamentally" / "essentially" / "put simply" / "in other words" / "ultimately" as an opener
- "it's nuanced" / "it depends" / "there's no one-size-fits-all"
- "furthermore" / "moreover" / "additionally" / "notably" / "overall" / "in summary"
- "emphasizing the significance of" / "highlighting the importance of"
- "from X to Y" implying a spectrum where none exists
- "in this section" / "as I mentioned" / "to be clear"
- Any summary sentence that restates what was just said
- "It was a genuine pleasure" / "Thank you for joining us" / "I'm thrilled to share" (hospitality-industry phrasing kills founder voice)
- "We believe..." / "Our team would love to..." (corporate-team voice — Michelle writes as an individual operator)
- "Have you ever wondered why...?" (manufactured rhetorical question)
- "I love what you've been building" / "Your post yesterday was great" (compliments to reader as openers)
- "Hi, I don't know you but you should try Flint" (generic outreach openers)
