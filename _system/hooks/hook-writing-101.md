# Hook Writing 101

The only goal of the hook is to make the reader stop scrolling and press "see more."
Any word that doesn't serve that goal gets cut.

You do this by creating a **curiosity gap**: the gap between what they know and what they're curious to know after reading your hook.

The hook must sound natural when read out loud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post, rewrite it.

---

## Method 1a — Build Authority

People won't read if they have no reason to trust you. Build that trust in the hook using numbers related to time, money, or volume.

Examples:
- I'm the CEO of a 20-person startup. Here's how we build culture without boring team-building activities:
- I spent 7 years in Big 4 consulting before starting [Company]. Here are 4 lessons I learned that carried over:
- I've sent 375,000 cold emails this week. Here are our top 3 best-performing templates:

---

## Method 1b — Use Specific Numbers

The more specific the number, the better. Always use the 2) version:

- ~~Last month we made 6 figures.~~ → Last month we made $151,323.60.
- ~~I've built dozens of websites in the last 2 years.~~ → I've built 37 websites in the last 22 months.
- ~~I sent thousands of cold emails last month.~~ → I sent 10,452 cold emails last month.

**Rule: Never use round numbers in hooks. Always use ultra-specific figures.**

---

## Method 2 — Start With A Story

Stories create curiosity gaps naturally. Compare:
- "Cold email is working really well for us right now." (bad)
- "Last month we tried a growth strategy I NEVER thought we'd try. It changed our business forever:" (good)

- "Here's an answer on if you should be posting every day:" (bad)
- "Yesterday, my biggest client asked me a question every Founder wants the answer to:" (good)

The story must be incomplete. The reader needs to feel they're missing something.

---

## Method 3 — Use : and …

Use these in 99% of hooks. They signal there's more to read.

The sentence BEFORE the colon or ellipsis must create the curiosity gap. If it doesn't, they're useless.

---

## Method 4 — Highlight A Process

When a client has a process that achieves a result:
- Lead with the end result
- Use "here's how" or "how…" to signal the process follows
- Include time frame and step count when possible

Templates:
- My [N]-step process for [end result] in [time frame]:
- I [achieve end result] in [time frame] using my [N]-step process. Here's how it works:
- I [achieve end result] in [time frame] without [pain point] using this [N]-step process:

---

## Method 5 — Promise A Listicle

Rules:
- Never promise 2 or fewer items
- Use odd numbers: 3, 5, 7, 9, or exactly 10
- Combine with authority whenever possible

Examples:
- I send 375,000 cold emails per week for 150+ offers. Here are 3 cold email mistakes I NEVER let my team make:
- I'm the CEO of a 27-person remote startup. Here are 9 rules for leadership I wish I knew on Day 1:

Variants:
- If you want [desired benefit], here are 7 [actions] you MUST do:
- If you want to avoid [pain point], here are 7 things you MUST avoid:

---

## Method 5a — Negativity Bias

Negative angles outperform positive ones. When deciding how to frame a post, try the negative version first.

Rules:
- Don't exaggerate: "these 7 mistakes are driving up CAC" not "destroying your business"
- Don't do it every post — be selective

---

## Method 6 — Make A Bold Statement

Three types:
1. Hot take / contrarian opinion: "Remote work sucks."
2. Something unexpected from you: "75% of Founders shouldn't post on social."
3. A firm stance: "Cold outbound is dead." (not "Cold outbound is getting harder.")

Rules:
- Can't do it too often or you become "the hot take person"
- Whatever follows must justify the hook — no bait and switch

---

## Method 7 — Borrow Authority

Reference a well-known brand or person — only when genuinely relevant.

Examples:
- Ramp is worth $7.5B. They send 25,000 cold emails/month. I got an inside look at their strategy — 3 takeaways:
- OpenAI is worth $150B. I studied their landing page and found 7 conversion hacks they're using:
- Airbnb MASTERED founder-led content. I broke down their strategy into 3 shockingly simple steps:

Often combines well with listicle hooks.

---

## The Reader Test

After writing a hook, ask: Would the reader have any reason to read the second line if the first line wasn't there?

If yes → the hook is working.
If no → rewrite.

## Topic Sources (for Idea Scout)

- Conversations with clients / their sales calls
- Industry trends and news
- Customer success stories
- Internal processes and frameworks
- Founder's personal experiences
- Competitive analysis
- Common customer questions and objections
- Reddit hot + top posts in relevant subreddits
